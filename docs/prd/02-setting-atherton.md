# 02 — Setting: Atherton

The research dossier. Everything the art, audio and NPC teams (or the one person wearing all three hats) need
to know about the real place. Compiled from public web sources; the caveats in the
[landmark register](../reference/atherton-landmark-register.md) apply throughout.

---

## The town in one paragraph

Atherton is a former mill-and-mining town of roughly 22,000 people in the Metropolitan Borough of Wigan,
Greater Manchester — 5 miles south of Bolton, 7 miles east of Wigan, 10 miles northwest of Manchester. For
three centuries it was better known as **Chowbent** (locals still use "Bent"). It made nails, spun cotton, and
dug coal from the Manchester Coalfield until the last deep pit closed in 1966. What's left is a compact
red-brick town along two crossing A-roads, a market, a dense pub run on Market Street, some genuinely old
buildings hiding in plain sight, and the physical remains of the industry that built it.

## History that survives into the modern townscape

The game is set today, so history matters only where it's *visible*. It is, in layers:

| Era | What happened | What you can still see |
|---|---|---|
| 1600s | Yeoman-farmer prosperity | **Alder House** (1697), **Chanters Farmhouse** (1678, "WA 1678" on the lintel) — both Grade II\*, both still standing among modern housing |
| 1721 | Nonconformist tradition builds its chapel | **Chowbent Chapel**, Grade II\*, the oldest place of worship in town, still active Unitarian |
| 1700s–1800s | Nail-making, then cotton | The name of the **Jolly Nailor** pub; mill-era street grain |
| 1800s | Coal at scale — Gibfield, Howe Bridge, Chanters pits | **Howe Bridge model village** (colliery-built terraces), the **1908 Mines Rescue Station** on Lovers Lane (first in Lancashire), **Gibfield pithead baths** (1913, first in the UK) |
| 1879 | Church rebuilding | **St John the Baptist's** — third church on a site used since 1645 |
| 1850s–1900 | "Atherton" displaces "Chowbent" as the official name | The dual naming that locals still navigate by |
| 1900s | Consolidation, decline of the pits (all closed 1959–1966) | Cleared colliery sites — Gibfield is now a business park |
| 1974 | Absorbed into Metropolitan Borough of Wigan | **Atherton Town Hall** (Bolton Road) demoted from council HQ to community hub and library |
| 1987 | Rail revival | **Hag Fold station**, built by British Rail for the estate |
| 2018 | Formby Hall demolished | A cleared site; the **Bent 'n' Bongs Beer Bash** moved to the Atherton Roller Rink |

The design takeaway: Atherton's texture is *layered utility* — a 1697 stone house sits on a street of 1900s
terraces near a 1980s estate, and nobody thinks it's strange. The art direction should resist the urge to
theme any district. See [09](09-art-and-audio-direction.md).

## Geography

- **Terrain:** low-lying, clay soil; ~30 m above sea level in the southwest rising to ~76 m in the north. A
  real, walkable, visible gradient.
- **Water:** Shakerley Brook forms the western boundary. Chanters Brook cuts through "The Valley."
- **Rail:** the Manchester–Southport line crosses the town east–west with stations at Atherton and Hag Fold.
- **Roads:** the A577 (Market Street/Mealhouse Lane, Wigan–Bolton) crosses the A579 (Bolton Road, the old
  Bolton–Leigh main road). The town grew along the A579; it shops along the A577.

Full details in the [street register](../reference/atherton-street-register.md).

## Districts and their character

| District | Feel |
|---|---|
| **Town centre / Chowbent** | Victorian commercial spine. Two- and three-storey red-brick shopfronts, the market, the pub run, the precinct. Busy daytime, lively evening, quiet 3 am. |
| **Howe Bridge** | The set-piece. A planned colliery village — uniform terraces, institute, church — with the strongest architectural coherence in town. |
| **Hindsford** | Workaday residential, church-anchored (St Anne's — redundant; Sacred Heart), running down toward Leigh. |
| **Hag Fold** | Post-war social housing estate with its own small station. Different grain from the Victorian core — cul-de-sacs, greens, pebbledash. |
| **Atherleigh / Lately Common** | Southern and peripheral edges, semi-rural fringe. |

## Culture worth encoding

These become NPC behaviours, audio, and set dressing — the things that make it *Atherton* rather than
Generic Northern Town:

- **The pub run.** Market Street's density of pubs within a few hundred metres — Jolly Nailor, Wheatsheaf,
  Punch Bowl, the Taphouse, the Lamp, Tiki Bar, Pound Pub, and the Pendle Witch just off it — is the town's
  social engine and the game's evening centre of gravity.
- **Bent 'n' Bongs Beer Bash.** The annual beer festival (30+ editions), now at the Roller Rink. Represented
  as interior dressing and posters — the festival itself is a possible future event feature.
- **Two non-league football clubs** for a town of 22,000: Atherton Collieries A.F.C. (founded 1916 by miners
  from the six pits, Northern Premier League Div One West, at Alder House) and Atherton Laburnum Rovers
  (North West Counties Premier, at Crilly Park). Matchday crowds are a schedule event.
- **The market.** Traditional outdoor market, trading days TBC (register, unresolved item 6).
- **"Bent."** The local name survives in institutions (Bent 'n' Bongs, Chowbent Chapel). Signage and NPC
  barks can use it naturally.
- **Accent and dialect.** Wigan-borough Lancashire, not Manchester. If NPC voice ever gets recorded or
  generated, this matters and is easy to get wrong.

## Sensory reference (for art and audio)

- Red brick, stone-slate and Welsh-slate roofs, pebbledash on the estates, UPVC everywhere.
- Overcast as the default sky. Greater Manchester light is diffuse; harsh sun is the *rare* weather state.
- Trains passing on the embankment; the Bolton Road traffic wash; gulls; market-day PA and chatter.
- Vegetation: hawthorn, sycamore, bramble along the brooks and the old pit land; Colliers Wood's 5 km of
  trails through reclaimed colliery ground.
